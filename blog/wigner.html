Wigner's Friend and Schrödinger's Observer

May 2026

The standard telling of Schrödinger's cat, in pop science and in a lot of textbooks as well, goes roughly like, a cat is placed in a sealed box with a quantum device, and until you open the box, the cat is simultaneously alive and dead in superposition. Observation collapses the wavefunction into one definite outcome.

But this is almost exactly backwards from what Schrödinger intended.

In his 1935 paper in Naturwissenschaften, Schrödinger was constructing a reductio ad absurdum. He devised the cat in 1935 as a thought experiment to expose what he viewed as the utter absurdity and flaws of the prevailing "Copenhagen interpretation" of quantum mechanics If you apply the superposition principle to macroscopic systems without restriction, you arrive at a conclusion he described as "quite ridiculous" such as a cat simultaneously alive and dead. His point was NOT as seen as it is today on "look how strange quantum mechanics is" but something closer to "this cannot be right and the theory, as stated, does not match what any observer actually experiences." The cat was rather a symptom of an unresolved foundational problem.

That original concern was about the experience of an observer inside a quantum process and not outside it, but this turns out to be exactly the right thread to pull on when thinking about Wigner's Friend. And the direction it leads, I think, is more clarifying than most of the existing treatments suggest.

1. Wigner's Friend

In 1961, Eugene Wigner formalized the tension Schrödinger had identified. Consider a friend who performs a quantum measurement inside a sealed laboratory. The system being measured is a spin-\(\frac{1}{2}\) particle prepared in an equal superposition of up and down:

$$|\psi\rangle_\mathcal{S} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(|\uparrow\rangle + |\downarrow\rangle\bigr)$$

The friend interacts with this system via a measurement apparatus. Under the von Neumann model, unitary evolution maps the initial product state to an entangled state:

$$|\psi\rangle_\mathcal{S} \otimes |A_0\rangle \;\xrightarrow{U_{\mathrm{int}}}\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(|\uparrow\rangle|\text{friend sees }\uparrow\rangle + |\downarrow\rangle|\text{friend sees }\downarrow\rangle\right)$$

From the friend's position, she observes one definite outcome, say, spin-up. So she knows the result. But now from Wigner's position, standing outside the sealed laboratory, the formalism permits him to describe the entire lab, with his friend included, as remaining in the entangled superposition above. He has not interacted with the lab and has received no information about the outcome. Standard quantum mechanics does not instruct him to assign a definite value to the friend's measurement record.

The apparent contradiction here is that the friend reports a definite experience, and Wigner's description shows no definite outcome. If quantum mechanics applies universally to all physical systems, including friends in laboratories, these two descriptions seem to be in tension and that is why this is a paradox.

1. Frauchiger and Renner (2018) converted this tension into a formal theorem where a specific set of assumptions about quantum universality, consistency of agent reasoning, and single-world outcomes leads to a logical contradiction in a carefully constructed nested scenario. Bong et al. (2020) derived a "no-go theorem for local friendliness" from similar premises, with photonic experimental tests. The present discussion addresses the original Wigner scenario; the Frauchiger–Renner extension is addressed in Section 7.

2. The Inside View

Here is a different way to enter the problem, a nested thought experiment (nested inside Schrödinger's Cat experiment). Forget, for a moment, the external view. Set aside Wigner standing outside and imagine you are the friend, inside the sealed laboratory, and you have just made the measurement. Now, what do you experience?

You experience one definite outcome which is not a superposition and not "both simultaneously" or "neither until observed." One result, immediately, without ambiguity from your position and Your stream of experience is continuous and definite.

This is the starting observation of what I want to call the Schrödinger's Observer: Consider the cat's perspective. If you are Schrödinger's cat, you are not suspended in some blurred superposition of alive and dead, waiting for a human to open the box and decide your fate. From inside the box, the cat is a macroscopic system embedded in an environment, coupled to air molecules and thermal radiation and its own biochemistry at every moment. The instant the quantum event occurs, the atom decays or it doesn't, the cat's state becomes entangled with that outcome through a cascade of irreversible physical processes. The cat here, experiences one definite reality. If it is alive, it is alive continuously, with no gap or blur, long before anyone outside touches the latch. The external observer's uncertainty about the outcome is real, and the quantum formalism permits them to write a superposition but that is a description of their non-definitiveness. The cat was never waiting for permission to be one thing or the other.

This is something you need to consider when people speak about superposition of anything. Qubit are superposition of 0 and 1? It is the gap between non-definitiveness of an unmeasured state and the definitiveness of a measured state and this gap is called the "measurement problem". It asks how a quantum system that exists in a probabilistic state of multiple possibilities (a superposition) instantaneously "collapses" into a single, definite reality when it is observed or measured What the Schrödinger's Observer framing does is make this the starting point rather than "the explanandum". You do not need to derive that you will have a definite experience from some more fundamental principle because you observe, immediately, that you do. The question then becomes "what does the quantum formalism say from this position, and how does it relate to what Wigner can consistently say from outside?"

3. Decoherence and the Internal Observer

Any realistic measurement involves more than a system and an apparatus. It involves an environment i.e. the thermal photons in the lab, the air molecules around the apparatus, the molecular collisions inside any warm material object. Including the environment \(\mathcal{E}\), the post-measurement state extends to:

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(|\uparrow\rangle|A_\uparrow\rangle + |\downarrow\rangle|A_\downarrow\rangle\right)\!\otimes|E_0\rangle \;\xrightarrow{U_{\mathrm{SE}}}\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(|\uparrow\rangle|A_\uparrow\rangle|E_\uparrow\rangle + |\downarrow\rangle|A_\downarrow\rangle|E_\downarrow\rangle\right)$$

This is still unitary evolution with no interpretational assumption. The environmental states \(|E_\uparrow\rangle\) and \(|E_\downarrow\rangle\) become rapidly orthogonal because each involves macroscopically distinct configurations of enormously many degrees of freedom. Once \(\langle E_\uparrow | E_\downarrow \rangle \approx 0\), the reduced density matrix of system and apparatus, obtained by tracing out the environment, becomes:

$$\rho_{\mathcal{SA}} = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{E}}\!\left[|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\right] \approx \frac{1}{2}\!\left(|\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|\otimes|A_\uparrow\rangle\langle A_\uparrow| + |\downarrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|\otimes|A_\downarrow\rangle\langle A_\downarrow|\right)$$

The off-diagonal coherence terms have gone, the global state remains pure and entangled and the local state of system and apparatus becomes effectively mixed. This is what is called environmental decoherence, a direct consequence of unitary evolution applied to a system that cannot be isolated from its surroundings.

Decoherence shows that interference between macroscopically distinct outcomes becomes negligibly small in practice, immeasurably so; but it does not, without further interpretational commitments, select one outcome over another or resolve the question of whether both outcomes persist in some sense. Decoherence is not the same thing as collapse, and conflating them obscures the structure of what remains unsolved.

Timescales

The decoherence timescale can be estimated from the rate of environmental scattering. For thermal photon scattering from an object of size \(a\) at temperature \(T\), with distinguishable states separated by \(\Delta x\), the characteristic decoherence time is of order (Joos & Zeh 1985; Schlosshauer 2007):

$$\tau_D \;\sim\; \frac{\hbar^2}{m\,\sigma\,k_B T\,\Delta x^2 \cdot n_{\mathrm{photon}}}$$

where \(\sigma \sim \pi a^2\) is the scattering cross-section and \(n_{\mathrm{photon}} \sim (k_BT/\hbar c)^3\) is the thermal photon number density. For a macroscopic object at room temperature, \(T = 300\) K, \(m \sim 1\) kg, \(a \sim 10\) cm, \(\Delta x \sim 1\) cm, substituting standard constants gives:

$$\tau_D \;\sim\; \frac{(10^{-34})^2}{(1)(10^{-2})(1.4\times10^{-23})(300)(10^{-2})^2\cdot10^{13}} \;\sim\; 10^{-40}\;\text{s}$$

With partial shielding and more conservative parameters, Schlosshauer (2007) and Joos & Zeh (1985) obtain \(\tau_D \lesssim 10^{-20}\) s as a realistic upper bound for macroscopic superpositions. The shortest neural timescale relevant to perception is of order milliseconds and the gap between decoherence timescale and perceptual timescale is approximately twenty orders of magnitude.

The probability of spontaneous recoherence, of the environmental entanglement reversing itself, scales as:

$$P_{\mathrm{recoh}} \;\sim\; \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta S}{k_B}\right) \;\sim\; \exp\!\left(-10^{23}\right)$$

where \(\Delta S \sim k_B \times 10^{23}\) is the entropy increase from encoding the outcome into \(\sim 10^{23}\) environmental degrees of freedom. This is not "extremely unlikely" in the ordinary sense but it is thermodynamically prohibited by the second law.

2. Wigner's superposition description of the lab is formally permitted by the quantum formalism because it represents his epistemic state about a system he has not observed. But it cannot be operationally tested through interference experiments on the laboratory because doing so would require reversing the environmental entanglement accumulated over the decoherence timescale, which is thermodynamically not acheiveable.

4. Two Descriptions

The apparent contradiction rests on the assumption that both descriptions must simultaneously hold of some single, observer-independent state of affairs, that there is a "fact of the matter" about the lab which one of them must be getting wrong.

But what the quantum formalism implies, when applied consistently to observers in different positions, is that state assignments depend on what information is available to the observer making the assignment. The friend's state assignment reflects her direct participation in the measurement event and Wigner's reflects his epistemic situation that he knows the lab contains a friend who performed a measurement, but he has received no information about the outcome. These are descriptions from different observational contexts and there is no requirement that they agree.

This claim is the core of Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics (1996), and variants of it appear in QBism and consistent histories. What Schrödinger's Observer adds is a shift in starting point that rather than arriving at the observer-relativity of state assignments through philosophical argument, you begin from the phenomenological fact of definite internal experience and work outward. The physics is the same but the order of reasoning is different, and I think it makes the structure of the apparent paradox considerably clearer.

When Wigner eventually opens the lab and observes at time \(t_1\), he becomes entangled with the outcome:

$$|\Psi\rangle \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left( |\uparrow\rangle|\text{friend saw}\;\uparrow\rangle|\text{Wigner sees}\;\uparrow\rangle \;+\; |\downarrow\rangle|\text{friend saw}\;\downarrow\rangle|\text{Wigner sees}\;\downarrow\rangle \right)$$

From Wigner's position after observation, he has a definite outcome. His description and the friend's are now mutually consistent as they agree on which result occurred. No one created the friend's prior experience but Wigner became correlated with an outcome the friend had already observed, through ordinary physical interaction.

5. The Observer Chain

Now, to see is another way, add a third observer to Schrödinger's cat experiment , Alice, watching Bob perform the Schrödinger experiment from outside. Alice never opens the box and before she learns anything from Bob, standard quantum mechanics permits her to describe the composite system (Bob + box) as:

$$|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{Alice}} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left( |\text{Bob sees awake}\rangle|\text{awake}\rangle \;+\; |\text{Bob sees asleep}\rangle|\text{asleep}\rangle\right)$$

This is Alice's description of a system she has not yet obtained information about. It does not assert that Bob is in superposition in any experiential sense but Bob opened the box, became correlated with the outcome, and has a definite result from his position. Alice's state assignment to Bob reflects her ignorance, not Bob's uncertainty.

The temporal structure of information acquisition:

  • \(t_0\): Quantum event occurs. Inside observer has a definite experience.
  • \(t_1\): Bob opens the box, becomes physically correlated with the outcome.
  • \(t_2\): Alice learns from Bob, becomes physically correlated with the outcome.

At no point does any observer's acquisition of information reach backward and create definiteness for those earlier in the chain. Alice's observation does not create Bob's earlier definite experience and Bob's observation does not create the inside observer's earlier definite experience and hence the sequence runs strictly forward in time, following the natural direction of physical entanglement.

There is no privileged moment where reality "becomes real" for everyone. There is only a temporal sequence of physical systems becoming correlated with a measurement outcome through direct interaction. Each observer's description of systems they have not yet interacted with can include superposition which that is what the formalism permits, and it is an accurate representation of their epistemic state but it is not a claim that the earlier observers lack definite experiences.

3. One might ask whether this generates an infinite regress. Could there be a fourth observer who describes Alice as in superposition, and so on? Yes, and the same logic applies at each level. The chain can extend indefinitely but what it never requires is a final observer who validates everyone else. Each state assignment reflects a particular observational context. The regress does not demand a terminus.

6. Implications

To separate what follows from the formalism and established physics from what requires additional interpretational commitments:

What follows:

The von Neumann measurement chain is a mathematical consequence of unitary evolution applied to composite systems with no interpretational assumption required and environmental decoherence (suppression of off-diagonal coherences) follows from unitary evolution applied to open systems. Decoherence timescales for macroscopic objects at room temperature are of order \(10^{-20}\) s, many orders of magnitude below any neural or observation timescale (Joos & Zeh 1985; Schlosshauer 2007). So, Any interference experiment that would operationally test Wigner's superposition description would require reversing environmental entanglement; the probability scales as \(\exp(-10^{23})\), thermodynamically not acheiveable. And different observers can consistently assign different quantum states to the same system, as the formalism implies when applied from different observational positions.

What requires additional interpretational commitments

  • That the friend "has a definite experience" in any precise sense, connecting the reduced density matrix to phenomenology requires a bridge principle not present in the formalism itself.
  • That quantum state assignments are intrinsically relative to observers rather than objective. This is the central claim of relational quantum mechanics (Rovelli 1996), not a consequence of the math.
  • That the apparent contradiction in Wigner's Friend is fully dissolved by perspectival reasoning but whether this constitutes dissolution or reframing depends on what you think the paradox was originally asking.
  • That all interpretations are equally tenable here. Consciousness-collapse interpretations are in direct tension with this framework; Many-Worlds, Relational QM, and Quantum Darwinism are broadly compatible.

The minimal commitments the Schrödinger's Observer framework makes are that quantum state assignments represent information available to a particular observer, and that environmental decoherence explains why the internal observer's perspective is stable and effectively irreversible. These commitments are compatible with several interpretations of quantum mechanics but not all. So claiming the framework is interpretation-neutral would be inaccurate.

7. The Frauchiger–Renner Scenario

Frauchiger and Renner (2018) extended Wigner's scenario into a protocol involving nested observers that derives a logical contradiction from three premises: that quantum mechanics applies universally, that measurement outcomes are facts observers can consistently reason about, and that observers can apply quantum mechanics to reason about other observers as quantum systems.

The protocol requires, crucially, that an external observer perform an interference experiment on a system that includes a macroscopic observer who has already made a measurement. In physical terms, you must reverse the decoherence of a warm macroscopic system on a laboratory timescale. The probability of this scales as \(\exp(-10^{23})\), as computed above. The contradiction in Frauchiger–Renner requires, as one of its operational steps, something thermodynamically not achievable.

Experiments by Proietti et al. (2019) and Bong et al. (2020) have tested related scenarios using photonic qubits as stand-ins for observers. But, they do not imply that macroscopic conscious observers experience superposition, because photonic qubits can be maintained in coherence precisely because they are not warm macroscopic objects interacting with \(\sim 10^{23}\) environmental degrees of freedom.

Conclusion

This started from a fairly simple question of why does Wigner's Friend seem paradoxical when the ingredients for understanding it, like environmental decoherence, observer-relative state assignments, etc. have been in the literature for decades? Part of the answer is that the popular framing of Schrödinger's cat has trained people to think of observers as external to the systems they observe, waiting to collapse wavefunctions by looking. Schrödinger's Observer is an attempt to correct for that framing, by starting from inside rather than outside. Whether it fully dissolves the paradox or only reframes it is something I have left as an honest open question.

Schrödinger was right to be troubled by the cat because applying it without attention to whose description you are writing leads to apparent absurdity.

With Schrödinger's Observer, the apparent contradiction in Wigner's Friend becomes less mysterious once you recognize that the friend's definite experience and Wigner's superposition description are not competing accounts of a single objective state; they are descriptions from different observational contexts, both permitted by the formalism, and there is no requirement that they agree. When Wigner eventually opens the lab, his description and the friend's become mutually consistent and nobody created anyone else's experience. The sequence of observers acquiring information ran forward in time, through ordinary physical entanglement.

(2020) for the extended no-go results; Zurek (2003) for decoherence, einselection, and quantum Darwinism.
